Ace was already effective. The challenge was making consumers notice, understand and choose it.

A mixed-method market research project developed in response to a real business challenge from Fater, combining consumer research, behavioural observation, text analysis and strategic recommendations.

01

Business challenge

Understanding the real business problem

Before proposing communication, we needed to understand what was holding the product back.

The initial objective was ambitious: help Ace Spray become the category leader within five years. Before proposing new communication, we needed to understand whether the real barriers came from product performance, price perception, brand awareness, packaging or changing consumer expectations.

What was preventing Ace Spray from becoming the category leader, despite its product strength and market presence?

Four core insights

A strong product was being held back by how consumers perceived, recognised and experienced it.

01
Mental availabilityPurchased ≠ rememberedChanteclair dominated top of mind; Ace was second among products purchased.

Product strength vs mental availability

What the data shows
Ace was purchased, but it was not equally present in consumers’ minds.
Why it matters
Usage and awareness were not aligned.
Business implication
Ace needed to strengthen mental availability, not only product performance.
02
PackagingToo much to processComplex information, limited shelf visibility and unclear destination of use created friction.

Packaging communicated too much and clarified too little.

“È un packaging sporco per un prodotto che deve pulire.”Comment from the designer focus group
What the data shows
Consumers found the packaging elaborate, difficult to read and less effective as a fast decision tool.
Why it matters
Packaging clarity, shelf visibility and ease of use were part of the product experience.
Business implication
Reduce visual complexity and turn the package into a faster decision tool.
03
Switching behaviour70% switch brands64% switch for more convenient prices. Loyalty: Chanteclair 39% · Ace 19%.

The category was highly switchable.

What the data shows
Price was not only a purchase variable. It was a switching trigger.
Why it matters
Strong category loyalty could be challenged when consumers saw a more convenient option.
Business implication
Promotions and bundles could attract consumers from the category leader and encourage trial.
04
Cleaning ritualMusic, podcasts, quick choicesSmartphone use was strong among younger consumers and product selection often happened quickly.

Cleaning was also an audio ritual.

What the data shows
Younger consumers often used music or podcasts while cleaning, while Ace needed greater awareness and stronger loyalty.
Why it matters
The opportunity was not simply to advertise during cleaning. It was to become part of the cleaning ritual.
Business implication
A physical product touchpoint could create a bridge to a more relevant digital experience.

Ace–Spotify packaging concept

Turning cleaning into a branded ritual

We did not start with a Spotify concept. We arrived there by following the consumer evidence.

Behavioural observation→Consumer insight→Physical touchpoint→Digital experience→Campaign concept

Consumer evidence

  • Younger consumers often used music or podcasts while cleaning.
  • Smartphone usage was dominant among Gen Z.
  • Product selection often happened quickly.
  • Ace needed greater awareness and stronger loyalty.

Strategic decision

Use the packaging as a bridge between the physical product and digital content.

Concept

  • Spotify code or QR-style interaction on the bottle.
  • Access to cleaning playlists.
  • Association between Ace and a recurring cleaning ritual.
  • Packaging used as a digital touchpoint.

This was a data-informed campaign concept. It was not a launched or officially approved Ace–Spotify partnership.

Packaging showcase

Redesigning the package as a decision and engagement tool

The concept was not purely aesthetic. It connected observed consumer friction to a clearer design response.

Consumer friction

Crowded packaging was difficult to read.

Simpler hierarchy and clearer destination of use.
Consumer friction

Ace was sometimes associated automatically with bleach and aggressiveness.

Prominent “without bleach” communication where relevant.
Consumer friction

The package was underused as a communication touchpoint.

Spotify interaction placed directly on the bottle.
Consumer friction

Consumers made decisions quickly.

Short, immediate copy and a visible interaction prompt.

Insight to decision

Evidence became a decision system, not a list of findings.

Insight

Young consumers often listen to music or podcasts while cleaning.

Decision

Turn the bottle into a digital touchpoint.

Concept: A playlist-enabled packaging experience.
Insight

Consumers choose quickly and find crowded packaging difficult to process.

Decision

Reduce cognitive load.

Concept: Clearer hierarchy and more immediate product purpose.
Insight

The category is price-sensitive and highly switchable.

Decision

Reduce the risk of trying Ace.

Concept: Bundles and targeted promotions.

Strategic recommendations

A balanced system across awareness, conversion, packaging and sustainability.

Awareness

  • Cleaning influencer collaborations
  • Ace–Spotify concept

Conversion

Packaging

Sustainability

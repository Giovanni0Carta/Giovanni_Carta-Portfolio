Key analytical tension: Revenue maximisation ≠ Profit maximisation. This was a university team project; all preference shares, revenues and profits are simulation outputs based on stated assumptions.

01 · Conjoint analysis

From product attributes to customer trade-offs

Attributes

Products are combinations of screen, sound, camera, dual SIM and price.

Utilities

Utilities represent relative value assigned to levels such as OLED versus LED or Stereo versus Mono.

Trade-offs

Customers choose complete bundles: better sound or a lower price?

Conjoint analysis does not ask which feature sounds attractive in isolation. It evaluates the trade-offs consumers make between complete alternatives.

02 · Choice simulation

Two ways to simulate choice

First Choice

Each respondent selects the product with the highest utility.

Winner-takes-all · more extreme · simple benchmark
Logit

Each product receives a probability based on relative utility.

Smoother · probabilistic · used for main project decisions
Utilities → exponentiation → normalisation → product probabilitiesP(product i) = exp(Ui) / Σ exp(Uj) · Simplified Logit representation.

Preference share is not the same as observed market share.

Variable price extension

Does the best configuration change when price can change?

At fixed $350, OLED + Stereo maximises simulated profit. When price can vary, OLED + Atmos at $400 produces $499.2M simulated profit — $12.2M above the fixed-price result.

Product configuration + pricing = joint decision.

Takeaways

Four lessons from the model

Lower price can create more demand, only within the defined simulated response.Sound is the most influential upgrade in this project’s utility structure.Revenue and profit lead to different product recommendations.Competitive reactions can materially change the preferred launch strategy.

Model assumptions

What the simulation assumes

Conjoint utilities, competitor configurations, a 10 million-smartphone market, prices and manufacturing costs are hypothetical inputs. Preference share is a demand proxy; the model does not include distribution, marketing, capacity, channel constraints, uncertainty intervals or real-market calibration.

My contribution

Connecting model output to a decision.

Market simulation logicScenario analysisRevenue and profit comparisonInterpretation of product trade-offsVisual communication of recommendations

This project strengthened my understanding that consumer preference models become valuable only when they are connected to pricing, cost and competitive decisions.

Limitations

Scenario evidence, interpreted with care.

Choice is simulated rather than observed. Preference share is not real market share; the competitive set, utilities, costs and market size are simplified and hypothetical. No consumer heterogeneity, confidence intervals, launch execution data or real-world validation is included.
